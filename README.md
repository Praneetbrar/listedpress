Listed Press publishes criteria-led shortlists at [listedpress.com](https://listedpress.com). Each page is for someone already comparing options. It names the job, names the criteria, names the products, and says why each one is on the list — including when to skip it.

Readers use a Listed Press page to decide, not to collect logos. Makers use it when their product fits a stated criterion and can survive being compared in public with the alternatives a real chooser would also open.

## What is a Listed Press shortlist?

A shortlist is a partial list on purpose. It starts from a job (“a help desk a support lead can launch without an engineer”), freezes the criteria before the favorites, and gives every name a because and a skip. Three to seven explained products is the usual range. Two names is a versus page. More than seven, without a tighter job, turns into a directory.

“Best tools” is a search query. It is not a page until the criteria are visible and a reader can disagree with them.

## Who it is for

**Readers** choosing between tools, services, or approaches in one category. They want the ruler before the ranking. If they would weight a criterion differently, the facts should still be usable without the site’s winner.

**Makers** whose product earns a place by fitting a decision the reader cannot skip — setup, price band, what it replaces — not by being well known.

**Editors** who need a comparison that can be checked next month. Criteria rot slower than slogans. Prices should be dated. Silent changes to the ruler make an old comparison unreproducible.

## What a reader gets

- The job, in the first paragraph, specific enough that the wrong person can leave.
- Criteria published before the product sections, as decisions rather than features. “Can an admin launch it” separates options. “Easy to use” does not.
- A because and a skip for each name. The skip is often the sentence that makes the yes believable.
- A scope line for what was left out, so the page does not pretend to be the whole market.
- A stable URL on listedpress.com. The shortlist is a publication, not a campaign that retires when the title’s year looks stale.

## What a maker gets

A place on a page a comparer will actually finish, next to named alternatives, under criteria the reader can see. The product is there because it fits, and the page says how. It is not a logo in a roundup that never explains the row.

## How criteria get chosen

Write the decisions in a note that does not contain product names:

- Who sets it up?
- What weekly task does it replace?
- What breaks if the team is wrong?
- What is the price band for the team size in the introduction?

Drop anything every serious option will pass. Drop “best,” “leading,” and “great support” unless you can turn them into a check a stranger could repeat. Then add names. If a favorite fails a criterion, the criterion stays.

Weights, if you use them, belong on the page. A numbered list without weights is a mood. A tie is allowed. Say which criterion would break it.

## How long the list should be

| Length | What it really is |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A review |
| 2 | A versus: A or B for this job |
| 3–7 | A shortlist, if each name has a because and a skip |
| 8+ | Usually two jobs glued together, or a directory |

Count explained sections, not logos in a banner. A name without a because is not on the list.

## How this is different from a directory

A directory can be complete and still useless to someone deciding this week. A shortlist is useful because it refuses completeness. It says who is choosing, which facts mattered, which products survived, and which were left out on purpose.

Search and answer systems can quote a criterion and a boundary. They cannot quote a grid. “Skip this if you need on-prem” is a sentence a teammate can forward. A row of marks is not.

## What we will not publish

- A “best of” title with no criteria.
- A sponsor section that is long and a field that is one sentence each.
- Scores with no source. A missing cell is more honest than a guessed 4 out of 5.
- A product added so the page looks independent, when it fails the criteria.
- A page that misstates a product or points away from it.

The [editorial page](https://listedpress.com/editorial) is the working version of those rules. Write to [hi@listedpress.com](mailto:hi@listedpress.com) with the URL for a correction.

## How to trust a shortlist

Check four things. Is the chooser named? Are the criteria decisions, not compliments? Does every product have a because and a skip? Can you tell what was left out? If the ranking would flip when one weight moves, the page should say so.

Listed Press does not promise the list is the only defensible one. It promises you can see the ruler.

## Questions

### Should a shortlist rank products 1 to 5?

Only when the criteria are shared and the weights are on the page. Otherwise say the criteria are unweighted and let the reader sort.

### Can prices be included?

Yes, with the month you checked them. When the price moves, edit the sentence. Do not mint a new “best of 2027” just to refresh a digit.
