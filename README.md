**A Listed Press shortlist states the job, the criteria, and why each named product is there — including when to skip it.** [listedpress.com](https://listedpress.com) publishes those pages for someone who is already comparing options. “Best help desks” is a search. It becomes a page only when a reader can disagree with the ruler before they disagree with the picks.

## A list you can argue with

Take a support lead choosing a queue for a team of ten. The page does not open with a winner. It opens with that person, then the decisions they cannot skip:

- Who can launch it without an engineer?
- Does it replace the shared inbox, or sit beside a help desk they already pay for?
- Is the price public for that team size?

Only then come the names. Each name gets a because tied to one of those decisions, and a skip. “Acme routes mail without a developer, which matters if the chooser is a support lead. Skip it if you need on-prem.” That pair is the section. “Acme is great for growing teams” is not.

Three to seven explained names is the usual range. Two names is a versus page, not a shortlist: the field is missing. One name is a review. More than seven, without a tighter job, is how a shortlist becomes a directory. The count is explained sections, not logos in a banner. [How many products belong on a shortlist](https://listedpress.com/blog/how-many-products-belong-on-a-shortlist) goes through the breaks.

## Criteria before favorites

Write the decisions in a note that has no product names. Drop anything every serious option will pass. Drop “best,” “leading,” and “easy to use” unless you can turn them into a check a stranger could repeat: time to first ticket, chat versus a forum, public price or a sales call.

Then add names. If a product you like fails a criterion, the criterion stays. Changing the ruler to save a favorite is how a shortlist becomes an advertorial with a grid. [How to choose comparison criteria](https://listedpress.com/blog/how-to-choose-comparison-criteria) is the full pass. [How to write a software shortlist people trust](https://listedpress.com/blog/how-to-write-a-software-shortlist) is the page-level version.

The [shortlist gap check](https://listedpress.com/tools/shortlist-gap) looks at the shape only: at least three products, at least three criteria, no duplicate names, no criterion that is only a compliment. A pass means the list can hold a decision. You still have to write the because.

## Weights, ties, and prices

If you rank, show the weights. A numbered list with hidden weights is a mood. The [criteria weighter](https://listedpress.com/tools/criteria-weighter) turns weights and 1–5 scores into a total. It will not pick the weights for you. If moving one weight by ten points swaps the winner, the page should say the result is sensitive.

A tie is allowed. Name the criterion that would break it. For two products and one job, the [versus outline](https://listedpress.com/tools/versus-outline) is a skeleton: the job, the criteria, a because and a skip for each side. It does not invent the facts.

Prices rot faster than criteria. If you print a price, date the month you checked it. When it moves, edit the sentence on the same URL. Do not mint a new “best of” title just to refresh a digit.

Say what you left out. “We skipped suites that need a sales call before a trial” tells the reader with that need to go elsewhere, and tells everyone else the page is not claiming the whole market.

## What gets refused

A “best of” title with no criteria. A long section for one product and a sentence for the rest. A score with no source. An empty cell is more honest than a guessed 4 out of 5. A surprise name that fails the criteria so the page looks independent.

Also refused: a page that misstates a product, or points away from it. The [editorial page](https://listedpress.com/editorial) is the rule. Corrections: [hi@listedpress.com](mailto:hi@listedpress.com) with the URL.

Listed Press does not promise you will pick the product on the list. It publishes the ruler, the names, and the skips, and leaves the page up so the comparison can be checked next month. Plans are on [pricing](https://listedpress.com/pricing). A product is submitted at [submit](https://listedpress.com/submit).

## Questions about shortlists and roundups

### Should I rank the products?

Only when the criteria are shared and the weights are on the page. Otherwise say the criteria are unweighted and let the reader sort. A forced winner is less useful than a tie with a lever.

### How many criteria?

Three to six decisions the reader cannot skip. More than six usually means two of them are features in disguise. “Has an API” is a feature. “A support lead can launch it” is a decision.

### Are “tools we tried” posts shortlists?

Only if you say the single axis is “we used it.” A diary is not a ranked comparison. Do not present one as the other.

### What if the market has more than seven real options?

Split by job. “Help desks a support lead can launch” and “help desks that replace a large install” are two shortlists. Merged, they are a directory with opinions.

## A shortlist in miniature

Job: a support lead at a ten-person company wants to stop triaging a shared inbox before standup. They already refuse anything that needs an engineer to launch, and they want a price they can read without a call.

Criteria, stated before the names: an admin can launch it; it replaces the shared inbox rather than adding a second queue; the price for ten people is public.

**Northstar.** Because an admin can connect the inbox and have a queue the same day, which matches the “no engineer” rule. Skip it if you need the tool to be the system of record for a hundred agents. It is a morning queue, not an enterprise suite.

**Ledger Mail.** Because the price for a team of ten is on the page, and the lead can compare it to the inbox they already pay for. Skip it if you need mail to stay in the old inbox and only want a summary. Ledger wants to be the inbox.

**Acme Inbox.** Because it is the familiar name a director may already have heard, and it does replace a shared inbox. Skip it if the launch needs an admin who is not an engineer — the trial assumes someone who can edit a DNS record and a webhook. That fails the first criterion. It stays on the list only so the reader can see the failure, not so the page can look complete.

Left out: suites that will not show a price, and products that only summarize mail without giving the lead a place to close a ticket. A reader who wants a summary layer, not a new queue, should not be talked into this job.

That is a shortlist. It can be argued with. A reader who weighs price above setup can prefer Ledger and say so, because the weights were not hidden. A reader who needs an engineer-friendly tool can take Acme and ignore the page’s lean. The facts survived the disagreement.

## How a list goes stale, and how to correct it

The dangerous edit is a silent one. A criterion disappears because a sponsor failed it. A price changes and the old number stays because rewriting feels like admitting age. A product sunsets and the section remains, cheerful and wrong.

Correct on the same URL. If a price moved, change the sentence and keep the month you checked. If a product no longer fits a criterion, change the because and the skip, or move it to the “left out” note and say why. If the job itself changed — you are now writing for a fifty-person team — that is often a new page, with a link from the old one, because the ruler is a different ruler. Pretending it is the same comparison strands anyone who bookmarked the original.

Readers forgive a dated fact that you fix. They do not forgive a table that invented a score. If you cannot source a cell, leave it empty and say the source was missing. “We think” is not a source. Your own use is a source if you say what you used it for and for how long.

## Who is actually comparing

A founder, an admin, and a director can all search “best help desk” and need different pages. The founder may care that a trial does not require a card. The admin may care about the DNS record. The director may care what breaks if the team is wrong in ninety days. One shortlist that tries to serve all three will grow criteria until nobody can hold them.

Name the chooser in the first paragraph and attach every criterion to that person’s week. Other people can still read it. They should be able to see, quickly, that the ruler is not theirs. That is kinder than a universal “best” that is secretly one person’s week written as a law.

Include one name the reader may not know, if it fits a criterion the famous names miss. Label it as the unfamiliar option and give it the same because and the same skip. A list of only household names is often a memory test. A surprise that fails the criteria, added so the page looks independent, is a mascot. Readers notice when the new name has no sourced fact.

## Rankings that move when you touch one number

Publish the weights if you publish an order. A reader should be able to redo the arithmetic. If setup is 50, price is 30, and “replaces the inbox” is 20, say that. If you would not defend those numbers to the chooser in the introduction, they are not your real weights. They are decoration on a favorite.

When a small change swaps the winner, do not hide it by rounding. Say the top two are close on this ruler, and name the criterion that separates them. Close results are useful. They tell a reader with a slightly different week where to push. A fake gap of “9.6 versus 7.1” built from moods is how comparison pages lost trust.

Yes/no criteria should stay yes/no. “Needs an engineer” is not a 4 out of 5. Scoring a binary fact makes the table look precise and makes it nonsense. Use a score only when the fact is a range you defined and kept stable, such as minutes to a first closed ticket in a trial you actually ran.

## More questions

### Can a shortlist be updated every month?

Yes, on the same URL, with the facts that moved called out. A new URL every month for the same job splits the citations and leaves old prices ranking beside new ones. Refresh the page. Do not clone it for the calendar.

### Where do affiliate relationships go?

In plain text, near the top or beside the product, not instead of the because. The reason the product is listed still has to be a criterion. A relationship that changes the because is an ad. Label it as an ad or take it off the list.

### What is the smallest honest comparison?

Three names, three criteria, each name with a because and a skip, and a sentence about who was left out. Fewer criteria is a preference. Fewer names is a versus or a review. Those forms are fine. They should not wear a shortlist’s title.

### Should the winner be in the title?

Only if you also show the ruler that made them the winner. “Best help desk” with no chooser and no criteria is a billboard. “A help desk a ten-person support lead can launch without an engineer” can have a lean toward one name and still be a page someone can use if they disagree.
